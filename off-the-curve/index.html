Ballon d'Or 2026 · Striker aging

Off the Curve

Harry Kane won the Ballon d'Or in the season he started at age 32. I built striker aging curves from 15 years of Big 5 league data to measure how far off the curve that is, and tested my own theory for why. The theory failed.

The question

Most aging curves say strikers peak in their mid-twenties and fade after 30. Kane just posted the best non-penalty scoring rate of any Big 5 forward season started at age 32 in the dataset, ahead of Lewandowski's 2021-22 (0.92) and Ronaldo's 2017-18 (0.91). Clubs price contracts and transfer fees off these curves, so a player this far above one is either a one-off or a sign the curve is wrong for his type.

My starting hypothesis: strikers who don't depend on dribbling and carrying age better, because acceleration is what leaves first. If true, a recruitment team should use a separate curve for "finishers" and pay more for them past 30.

Finding 1: The finisher theory is wrong

Low-carry finishers decline earlier, not later. By 29 they've lost 0.077 npG/90 relative to 27, while carriers are flat (-0.004), and the 80% intervals don't overlap at that age. By 32 the finisher curve sits at -0.111 (interval -0.16 to -0.07); carriers at -0.043 (-0.09 to +0.02). Past 30 the carrier sample thins out and the gap is inside the noise.

My read, not tested here: finishers' output is tied to chance volume, so it drops when a club demotes them or they move down a level. Carriers create part of their own shots and hold value longer. A "buy the poacher, he'll age well" rule isn't supported here.

Kane also isn't a clean finisher. On a composite of take-ons, progressive carries and carries into the box at ages 29 and under, he sits at the 47th percentile of 729 profiled forwards, almost exactly the median. The two-bucket split can't describe him, which is a limit of the method, not just of the theory.

Finding 2: Kane is off every curve

From a prime baseline of 0.56 npG/90 at Spurs (ages 24–28), both the finisher curve and the all-forward curve project about 0.45 at 32. He produced 0.98. Over 2,377 minutes, that gap is about 14 non-penalty Bundesliga goals the curve says shouldn't exist.

His two best rates of the last seven seasons both came after he turned 30, both at Bayern. That points at the move, not the body.

Finding 3: The move changed his chances

At Spurs (2017-18 to 2022-23) Kane averaged 0.47 npxG per 90. His first Bayern season jumped to 0.85, with average shot distance down from roughly 17 yards to 14.4. Better chances explain most of the step up. They don't explain all of it: he outscored his npxG in every season but one, so the finishing hasn't aged either.

Penalties sit on top. He scored 10 Bundesliga penalties in 2025-26, more than in any Spurs league season. Aging curves ignore them by design. Ballon d'Or voters don't, and penalty conversion doesn't fade with sprint speed.

What I'd tell a recruitment team

  • Don't model a 30+ striker on age alone. Model the chance environment he's moving into; a dominant side can more than offset the decline the curve predicts.
  • Don't assume low-dribble finishers age better. In this data they start declining around 28.
  • Profile on more than two buckets. Kane's mix of box finishing and deep creation is the profile worth its own curve, and two-way splits blur it.

Method

Data: FBref Big 5 league player-season tables, 2009-10 to 2024-25, via the worldfootballR_data release files. 6,114 forward seasons from 1,755 players (listed FW in most seasons, 900+ league minutes, seasons summed across clubs). Metric: non-penalty goals per 90. Age is age at season start, FBref convention.

Curves use the delta method: each player's change in npG/90 between consecutive qualifying seasons, weighted by the harmonic mean of minutes, averaged by age and chained. Ages with fewer than 15 pairs are dropped. Profiles: z-scored average of take-ons, progressive carries and carries into the penalty area per 90, from 2017-18 onward, using only seasons at age 29 or younger so aging doesn't leak into the label; median split. 2025-26 Kane figures (36 goals, 10 penalties, 2,377 minutes) come from bundesliga.com and StatMuse because the source extract stops at 2024-25.

Limits a skeptic should push on
  • Survivorship. The delta method only sees players who keep playing 900+ minutes. Strikers who fall off a cliff drop out, so every curve here understates decline after 30.
  • Small late samples. Only 47 carriers and 49 finishers have qualifying seasons at 31+. Treat the curves past 30 as direction, not size.
  • Profiles only exist from 2017-18, so profiled curves rest on 2017-25 transitions while the all-forward curve uses 2010-25.
  • No team-strength adjustment. Finding 3 suggests it matters; the next version should model npG/90 with team npxG share as a covariate.
  • League goals only. Kane's 61 all-competition goals and 6 World Cup goals aren't in the curve.
