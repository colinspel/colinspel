# Spending, Selling and Pressing: AS Roma under the Friedkins, 2020–2026

**How effectively has Roma turned money into results, and what should it do next?**

An analytical review combining public financial statements, wage data for every Serie A club, transfer records, expected-goals data and match statistics.

📄 **Full paper:** [AS_Roma_Analytical_Review.pdf](AS_Roma_Analytical_Review.pdf)

📱 **Roma Scout app:** [live app](https://colinspel.github.io/roma-scout/) · [code and details](scout/) — a matchday scouting tool with opponent dossiers, live table and game plans

## Key findings

1. **Wages set the expectation, and Roma underdelivered.** A log-wage model explains about two-thirds of the variation in Serie A points. Roma had a top-four wage bill but finished a combined 15 points below its wage-implied total from 2022/23 to 2024/25.
2. **Gasperini's third place was par, not an overachievement.** In 2025/26 Roma matched its wage expectation but finished about 7 points above what its xG difference predicted.
3. **Fee-based recruitment underperformed.** Only 3 of 13 signings costing €10m+ became core starters, while free agents Svilar and Ndicka are now valued at about €76m combined.
4. **The academy financed compliance.** Academy sales raised about €57m, but academy graduates' share of league minutes fell from 17.3% to 7.4%.
5. **Financial pressure persists.** Wages consumed 67% of operating revenue in 2024/25, and the Pietralata stadium is the biggest lever for closing the revenue gap.

The paper closes with a recruitment recommendation for Roma's weakest position.

**Sources:** Roma's published accounts, Calcio e Finanza, Sky Sport Italia, FBref / Opta and others (full list in the paper)
